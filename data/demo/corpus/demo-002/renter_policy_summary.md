# Minneapolis Home — Renter / Tenant Policy Summary (Sample)

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. A fictional, general summary of common landlord–tenant
> practices, written for a class capstone demo. It quotes no real statute and is
> not legal advice; tenant law varies by state and city. Renters should check
> their lease and their own local law.

Applies to: **1412 Larkspur Lane, Minneapolis, MN 55409** (the primary home). A
renter at the Dallas, TX home is covered by that home's own summary — the
cold-climate duties below, especially heat and snow, do not transfer to it.

## Security Deposits
Landlords commonly collect a security deposit of one to two months' rent. After
move-out the landlord typically returns the deposit, minus documented deductions for
damage beyond normal wear and tear, within a set period along with an itemized
statement. Photographs at move-in and move-out are the single most useful thing a
tenant can do to resolve a later dispute.

Normal wear and tear generally includes carpet traffic paths, minor wall scuffs, and
faded paint. It generally does not include pet damage, burns, large holes, or damage
from a preventable frozen pipe (see Heat below).

## Repairs and Maintenance
Landlords are generally responsible for keeping the unit habitable: working heat,
plumbing, electrical, hot water, and structural safety. Tenants are usually
responsible for keeping the unit clean, reporting problems promptly, and paying for
damage they cause. Repair requests should be made in writing and dated.

Urgent conditions — no heat in winter, no water, a sewage backup, an unsecured
entry door — are usually handled on a much shorter timeline than routine repairs.
Put the words "urgent" and the date in the written request.

## Heat in Winter
In a cold climate the landlord is normally responsible for providing a heating
system capable of maintaining a habitable indoor temperature, and for repairing it
promptly when it fails.

The tenant is normally expected **not to turn the heat off** while away. Setting the
thermostat back is fine; shutting it off during an Arctic outbreak is how pipes
freeze, and damage from that is usually charged to whoever caused it. A common lease
term requires a minimum setting of 55°F when the unit is unoccupied.

## Snow and Ice Removal
Responsibility for clearing snow from the driveway, steps and public sidewalk is set
by the lease, and it varies. In a single-family rental it is commonly the tenant's
duty; in a multi-unit building it is commonly the landlord's. Whoever holds it,
clearing is typically expected within 24 hours after snowfall ends.

If the lease is silent, ask in writing before the first snowfall rather than after a
fall on the walk.

## Entry and Notice
Landlords generally must give reasonable advance notice — often 24 hours — before
entering for a non-emergency reason such as an inspection, a showing, or a scheduled
repair. Emergencies, such as a burst pipe or a gas odour, do not require notice.

## Alterations, Drilling and Fixtures
Tenants generally may not make permanent alterations without written permission.
That includes painting, changing fixtures, mounting a television bracket, and
anything else that leaves anchors in the wall.

Hanging pictures with small nails or adhesive strips is usually acceptable, but the
lease governs. Where a tenant wants to hang something heavy, the practical options
are adhesive rail systems, over-the-door hangers, and leaning furniture-mounted
displays — none of which drill into the wall. Ask in writing and keep the reply.

## Yard, Landscaping and HOA Rules
A tenant in an HOA community is bound by the association's rules even though the
tenant is not a member of the association. Requests that need Architectural Review
Committee approval — fences, yard-surface changes, artificial turf, exterior colour
— must go through the **owner**, not the tenant. A tenant cannot apply directly.

## Ending the Tenancy
Month-to-month tenancies typically end on written notice from either side, commonly
one full rental period. Fixed-term leases usually end on their own date, and breaking
one early can leave the tenant responsible for rent until the unit is re-let. Give
notice in writing and keep proof of delivery.

## Moving Out
Leave the unit clean and empty, return every key, and provide a forwarding address in
writing — a deposit cannot be returned to an address the landlord does not have.
Do a final walkthrough with the landlord if possible, and photograph every room.
