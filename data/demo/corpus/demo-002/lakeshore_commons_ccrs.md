# Lakeshore Commons HOA — Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions (CC&Rs)

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. This is a fictional homeowners-association document created
> for a class capstone demo. It does not describe any real HOA, quote any real
> statute, or constitute legal advice. Section numbers are illustrative.

Applies to: **1412 Larkspur Lane, Minneapolis, MN 55409** (the primary home). The
Dallas, TX home is governed by a different association — do not apply these
covenants to it.

## 1. Purpose and Applicability
These covenants apply to all lots within the Lakeshore Commons community. Every
owner agrees to comply with these rules and with decisions of the Architectural
Review Committee (ARC). Tenants and short-term guests are bound by the same rules,
and the owner is responsible for their compliance.

## 2. Architectural Review Committee (ARC) Approval
Any exterior change to a home or lot that is visible from the street or from a
neighbouring lot requires written ARC approval **before** work begins. Submit a
written request describing the work, the materials, and the colours, with a simple
site sketch. The ARC responds within 30 days. Work started without approval may be
required to be removed at the owner's expense.

Changes that do **not** require approval: interior work of any kind, like-for-like
repair using the same material and colour, and seasonal decorations displayed for
fewer than 45 days.

## 3. Exterior Paint and Siding
Exterior colours must be selected from the approved palette on file with the ARC.
Repainting the same colour is a like-for-like repair and needs no approval.
Changing any colour, including trim and the front door, requires approval.

## 4. Roofing, Gutters and Ice Dams
Roof replacement requires ARC approval of the material and colour. Architectural
asphalt shingle in the approved earth-tone range is pre-approved.

Because ice dams are the community's most common winter claim, owners are expected
to keep gutters and downspouts clear each autumn and to have attic insulation and
ventilation maintained so that heat loss does not melt roof snow unevenly. Heat
cable is permitted on eaves and in gutters and does not require approval, provided
it is fastened neatly and the power feed is not run across a walkway.

Owners must not remove snow from a roof in a way that damages shingles or endangers
anyone below. Roof snow removal above the first storey should be done by an insured
contractor.

## 5. Driveways, Walkways and Snow
Driveways must be maintained free of significant cracking and heaving. Owners are
responsible for clearing snow and ice from their own driveway and the public
sidewalk fronting their lot within 24 hours after snowfall ends.

De-icing products containing magnesium chloride or calcium chloride are permitted.
Rock salt (sodium chloride) is discouraged on concrete less than one year old and
near planted beds, because of scaling and salt damage to plantings.

## 6. Landscaping and Yard Surfaces
Front yards must be maintained in a neat condition. Grass must be mowed during the
growing season and may not exceed eight inches.

**Replacing lawn with rock, stone, gravel or pebbles.** Owners may replace turf
grass in the **back yard** with decorative rock, stones, gravel or pebbles with
written ARC approval. Approval is granted routinely where the design retains
adequate drainage and does not direct runoff onto a neighbouring lot — which is the
committee's main concern in a spring-melt climate. In the **front yard**, full rock
or gravel replacement of the lawn is **not permitted**; rock may be used in planted
beds and borders, which is a common and readily approved design.

**Artificial turf.** Artificial turf is permitted in the back yard with written ARC
approval. The product must have a permeable backing so meltwater can drain, and it
must be installed over a prepared base rather than laid directly on soil. Artificial
turf is not permitted in the front yard.

**Trees.** Removing a healthy tree with a trunk diameter over six inches requires
approval. Removing a dead, diseased or storm-damaged tree does not, but the owner
should notify the ARC within 30 days.

## 7. Fences
Fences require ARC approval. Approved materials are cedar, composite, and black
powder-coated aluminium. Maximum height is six feet in the back yard and four feet
in any yard forward of the front building line. Chain-link is not permitted in any
location visible from the street.

## 8. Exterior Storage, Vehicles and Equipment
Trailers, boats, campers and recreational vehicles may not be stored on a lot for
more than 72 hours unless fully inside a garage. Refuse and recycling containers
must be stored out of view except within 12 hours of collection.

Snow-removal equipment may be stored outdoors between November and April if it is
placed beside or behind the home rather than in the front yard.

## 9. Antennas, Solar and Rooftop Equipment
Solar collectors are permitted with ARC approval of placement. The committee will
approve any placement that does not materially reduce output. Satellite dishes under
one metre are permitted and should be placed in the least visible workable location.

## 10. Accessory Structures
Sheds and detached structures require approval and must match the home's siding and
roofing. Maximum footprint is 120 square feet. Structures must sit at least five
feet from any lot line.

## 11. Pets and Animals
Common household pets are permitted. Livestock, poultry, and exotic or dangerous
animals are not permitted on any lot. Pets must be leashed in common areas, and
owners must remove pet waste promptly.

## 12. Nuisance, Noise and Quiet Hours
No activity may become a nuisance to neighbouring owners. Quiet hours are 10pm to
7am. Powered lawn and snow equipment may be operated outside quiet hours; an
exception allows snow clearing at any hour during an active snow emergency.

## 13. Leasing and Occupancy
Leases must be for a minimum term of 30 days. Rentals shorter than 30 days are
addressed in the community's short-term rental policy, which is a separate document.
Owners must give the association the name and contact details of any tenant within
14 days of a lease starting.

## 14. Enforcement and Fines
The association will give written notice of a violation and a reasonable period to
cure it, typically 14 days. Unresolved violations may draw a fine under the schedule
adopted by the board as **Resolution LC-2019-14**, which sets a first fine of $50, a
second of $150, and $300 for each 30-day period a violation remains uncured. Owners
may request a hearing before the board before any fine becomes final.

## 15. Maintenance Responsibility Summary
Owners maintain everything on their own lot, including the roof, siding, windows,
driveway, and all landscaping. The association maintains the entrance monument, the
walking path, the stormwater pond and its surrounds, and the common plantings along
the collector street.
