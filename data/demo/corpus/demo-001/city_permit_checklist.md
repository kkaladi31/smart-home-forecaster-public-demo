# Dallas Home — City Residential Permit Checklist (Sample)

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. A fictional city permitting checklist for a class capstone
> demo. Not real municipal guidance and not legal advice. Always verify with your
> actual local building department.

Applies to: **5000 Maple Street, Dallas, TX 75201** (the secondary home). These
thresholds do **not** apply to the Minneapolis, MN home — that home is in a cold
climate with its own frost-depth and egress requirements, and has its own checklist.

## When You Do NOT Need a Permit
Minor repairs and cosmetic work generally do not need a permit: interior painting,
replacing fixtures like faucets or a garbage disposal, flooring, cabinets, and
like-for-like repairs that do not alter structure, plumbing, gas, or electrical
capacity.

## Swimming Pool and Spa Permit
Any in-ground or above-ground pool holding water more than 24 inches deep requires a
building permit and an electrical permit. Requirements include a safety barrier/fence
at least 48 inches high with self-closing, self-latching gates, and an inspection of
bonding and grounding for pool equipment. Plans showing setbacks from property lines
and utilities must be submitted.

## Fence Permit
A permit is required for fences taller than 7 feet, or for any fence, of any height,
that serves as a required pool safety barrier. Fences under 7 feet that are not pool
barriers usually do not require a permit but must still meet HOA rules and setback
requirements.

## Water Heater Replacement Permit
Replacing a water heater requires a plumbing permit (and a gas permit for gas units).
The installation must include a temperature-and-pressure relief valve with a discharge
line, proper venting for gas units, and seismic strapping where required. An inspection
is required before the unit is fully enclosed.

## HVAC Replacement Permit
Replacing a furnace, air conditioner, or heat pump requires a mechanical permit.
Inspections verify safe electrical connections, condensate drainage, gas line and
venting integrity for combustion units, and correct equipment sizing.

## Deck and Patio Cover Permit
Decks more than 30 inches above grade, and any attached patio cover or pergola,
require a building permit with a structural plan. Ground-level patios (pavers or
concrete on grade) typically do not require a permit.

## How to Apply
Submit an application with a site plan and project details to the building department,
pay the permit fee, and schedule inspections at the required stages. Do not cover or
enclose work until it passes inspection. Permits are typically valid for 180 days and
can be extended.
