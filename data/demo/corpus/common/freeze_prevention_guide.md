# Cold-Weather Pipe & Freeze Prevention Guide

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. A fictional homeowner freeze-prevention guide for a class
> capstone demo, summarizing widely published cold-weather plumbing practices. Not
> professional advice; consult a licensed plumber for your specific home.

## When Pipes Are at Risk
Water freezes at 32°F (0°C). Pipe-freezing risk rises sharply when outdoor temperatures
fall to about 20°F (−6°C) or below, especially for pipes in unheated spaces (garages,
attics, crawlspaces, and along exterior walls). Wind and prolonged cold increase the
risk because they pull heat from pipes faster than still air.

## Before a Freeze
- Shut off the valve that supplies outdoor spigots, then open the spigots to drain them.
- Disconnect and drain garden hoses; store them indoors.
- Cover outdoor spigots with insulated faucet covers.
- Insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas with foam pipe sleeves.
- Know where your main water shutoff is, in case a pipe bursts.

## During a Freeze
- Let a pencil-thin stream of water drip from faucets served by exposed or exterior-wall
  pipes; moving water resists freezing.
- Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so heated indoor air reaches the pipes.
- Keep the home heated to at least 55°F (13°C), even when away.
- Keep garage doors closed if water lines run through the garage.

## If a Pipe Freezes
If a faucet produces only a trickle, a pipe may be frozen. Keep the faucet open, and
warm the pipe with a hair dryer or warm towels — never an open flame. If a pipe has
burst, shut off the main water supply and call a licensed plumber.

## Outdoor and Irrigation Systems
Before the first hard freeze, shut off and drain irrigation systems and backflow
preventers, and insulate any above-ground irrigation components. Drain and store
pool-equipment lines per the manufacturer's guidance if freezing is expected.
